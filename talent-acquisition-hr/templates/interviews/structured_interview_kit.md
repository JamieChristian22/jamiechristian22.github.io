# Structured interview toolkit: Talent Acquisition / HR Operations

Use a **consistent core question set** for the same role/stage and a documented, job-relevant evaluation rubric. The interviewer is responsible for interpreting evidence. This guide provides interview questions, evaluation anchors and accommodations considerations; it never automatically selects candidates.

## Interview agenda: 45 minutes

| Minutes | Segment | Interviewer responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–4 | Welcome / format / accessibility | Confirm accessible interview setup and expected time |
| 4–12 | Recruitment operations | Ask scenario 1 and record concrete evidence |
| 12–20 | Candidate communication | Ask scenario 2 and record concrete evidence |
| 20–30 | HRIS / data quality | Ask scenario 3 and clarify metrics |
| 30–39 | Confidentiality / conflict | Ask scenario 4, listen for escalation criteria |
| 39–45 | Candidate questions / next steps | Provide timing and authorized contact path |

## Four scenarios with observable evidence

**Q1 — High-volume scheduling:** You have six hiring managers, time zone conflicts and five candidates who need interview updates. How do you prioritize, coordinate and record actions? Strong evidence includes documented stakeholder dependencies, candidate fairness, accommodations, tracking and escalation.

**Q2 — Candidate experience:** An applicant has received conflicting information about next steps. How do you handle the mistake, give a concrete update and prevent repeat issues? Strong evidence includes ownership, factual communication, ATS note correction and preventive follow-up.

**Q3 — Reporting integrity:** An executive says the offer acceptance rate is 30% based on 24 accepted offers and 80 total offers, but 12 are pending. What is the correct denominator? Strong evidence includes excluding pending offers, documenting sample/period, reconciling source and explaining the difference. (Answer for this hypothetical: 24/(80-12)=35.3%; **not** the actual portfolio scenario.)

**Q4 — Sensitive information:** A panelist asks you to share applicant demographic answers to decide who moves forward. What action do you take? Strong evidence: decline inappropriate disclosure, separate voluntary data, document and escalate to authorized HR/Legal/Privacy; select only on validated job-related criteria.

## Anchored rating scale (no aggregate cut-off unless approved)

| Rating | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| 1 — Insufficient demonstrated evidence | Does not explain relevant steps, examples or safeguards |
| 2 — Partial evidence | Some appropriate steps, significant gaps in ownership, accuracy or escalation |
| 3 — Meets defined standard | Clear job-related approach with accurate steps, documentation and safeguards |
| 4 — Strong | Specific prioritization, stakeholder planning, error prevention and cross-team handling |
| 5 — Exceptional | Integrates reliable process design, candidate impact, controls, measurement and exception management |

**Rubric dimensions:** process planning, candidate experience, data accuracy, confidentiality and communication. Rate each separately after noting interview evidence. Require panelist name/ID, role, date and structured notes in approved ATS. Panel members must use approved and accessible questions; no requests for medical or family information.

## Quality gate before decision

- Were all candidates asked the same required core questions (with lawful accommodation where needed)?
- Is every rating supported by concrete job-related evidence instead of vague affinity statements?
- Are any discrepancies or conflicts documented and reviewed?
- Were unauthorized/private applicant details kept out of the rubric?
- Is the decision clearly owned by authorized human reviewers, with candidate notification recorded?
