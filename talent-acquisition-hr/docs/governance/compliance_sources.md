# U.S. hiring and HR compliance reference map (official sources)

**Last checked:** November 13, 2026. **Important:** this is an internal research starting point for a fictional business case, **not legal advice** or a legal compliance certificate. Rules vary by organization, state, city, industry, employee size, federal-contract status, protected class and evolving interpretations. Before any real use, an authorized HR professional and counsel must determine applicability and confirm the current law and agency guidance.

## Recordkeeping—careful scope boundaries

EEOC states that private employers covered by its recordkeeping rules generally retain covered personnel or employment records for **one year** after the record/decision, subject to relevant exceptions and longer requirements (including pending charges or litigation and other types of records). Payroll and benefits may require different timeframes. Do **not** use this portfolio as a universal deletion scheduler: DOL, state, federal-contractor, immigration and litigation hold requirements may override that general reference. Exact eligibility, clock trigger and hold rules require legal review.

For Form I-9, employer guidance generally uses **three years after hire or one year after employment ends, whichever is later**, with other special rules and exceptions as applicable; authorized employers should consult the official Form I-9 instructions and handbook. This demo purposely has **no I-9 forms, document images, SSNs or citizenship status data**. A handoff task is a process reminder, not proof of I-9 completion.

FCRA background checks obtained through a consumer-reporting agency require a specific written disclosure and written authorization before obtaining the report and specified notices before and after adverse action if report information forms the basis of that decision. This portfolio contains **no reports, authorization records, adverse action notices, background decisions or screening engine**. Such a process must be administered in secure, lawfully approved systems.

## Fairness and accessibility safeguards

- Select on job-related evidence and validated criteria; do not use cultural similarity, age proxies or disability assumptions.
- Offer appropriate accommodations and accessible assessments; route medical information to protected processes, not candidate scorecards or dashboard extracts.
- Demographic group information used for voluntarily submitted equal opportunity monitoring must be separately handled, access limited and not exposed to interviewers or hiring decision makers.
- The demo's Group A/B/C codes are artificial and are **not** official EEO categories or demographic declarations. The optional group rate report is a method exercise, not a formal adverse impact assessment.
- A four-fifths selection-rate screen is a **flag for further review**, not a finding of illegal discrimination or a statistical significance test. Real audits demand job-role/control factors, group counts, assessment validation and counsel involvement.
- Resume-screening AI, resume-matching scores, employment-related personality tests, automated decision systems or applicant-facing AI are **not implemented**. Evaluate any future use against EEOC/ADA, applicable state/local law and current NIST AI RMF.

## Jurisdiction-specific items intentionally excluded

Do not infer that a U.S.-wide repo resolves **state/city pay transparency, job posting statements, criminal-history/fair chance, salary-history limits, noncompete clauses, leave, disability accommodation, privacy notification, data subject access, electronic monitoring, AI hiring audit rules, or federal-contractor affirmative action obligations**. These change and must be individually scoped. Government guidance linked here may be updated; maintain a compliance-change register and legal owner in a real organization.
